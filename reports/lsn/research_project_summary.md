## Body (copy into the LSN post)

Hi scholars —

My Laidlaw project started as a stroke question. The governed file that arrived in August was not stroke.

It is monthly counts of the **first recorded hospitalisation after a first diagnosis** of coronary heart disease (CHD) or heart failure (HF) among people diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and/or hypertension in Hong Kong, January 2013–December 2023 (132 months). Admission cause is not recorded. Stroke was named in correspondence and has not been attached, so I am not reporting a stroke result.

That restraint is still the science. Earlier Hong Kong work on daily temperature and mortality (Jingwen Liu and colleagues, 2020) found a larger cold-attributable mortality burden than heat. Separate lab work models excess deaths under heatwave definitions. Our estimand is different again: **monthly thermal encodings and first-event morbidity counts**. Same city, related climate story, not interchangeable claims. First-event totals fall by about half across the decade while the general population aged 35+ rises — which is why these are count ratios, not incidence rates, until a still-at-risk cohort denominator exists.

The other lesson of this summer has been definitional honesty. “Hot month” is ordinary English and a methods problem. We catalogued many recipes, then ran a labelled panel on the file we actually have rather than fishing the catalogue. Age and sex splits never ran: the grain is territory-month. A 10 °C severe-cold recipe flagged zero months. Hogan leads the weather Methods; Roro leads the health-data construction; Professor David Bishai keeps the panel coherent. I am pasting the rest into a live shared manuscript so we stop emailing competing drafts.

If you want to *see* the weather half — mean monthly temperature, very hot days, hot nights, and cold days across eleven years, clearly labelled as **exposure descriptives, not health findings** — here is an interactive companion:
