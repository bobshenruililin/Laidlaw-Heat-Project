What the report already says

  • Admission cause was not recorded. Stroke was named and not attached; no stroke result is reported.
  • Model 1: twelve thermal comparisons, specified after the outcome series were available. After BH correction, all q > 0.19 (both leading estimates have q = 0.192).
  • Model 2 (monthly humidity and rainfall) and Model 3 (days above or below the historical same-calendar-day mean) are specified and not fitted in this report.
  • Exploratory CHD hot-night count ratio 1.022 (1.002–1.042) per five extra hot nights, under Newey–West lag 6; two of four uncertainty methods include 1; the association is not evident in 2013–2019.
  • Exploratory HF cold-day count ratio 1.073 (1.006–1.144) per five extra cold days; concordant across standard-error methods, still q-unprotected. Official cold days fall almost entirely in December–February (141 of 145).
  • The conclusion says CHD counts were more closely associated with hot nights, and HF counts with cold days, than with the other thermal measures. That ranking is not a multiplicity-protected difference, and it is not a journal headline.
