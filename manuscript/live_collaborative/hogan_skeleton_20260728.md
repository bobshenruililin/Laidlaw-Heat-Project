Introduction

Temperature-related cardiovascular morbidity is an important concern in subtropical cities, where populations may experience both intense humid heat and episodic cold despite generally warm climates. Acute myocardial infarction and stroke may respond differently to thermal exposure, and the observed burden can vary according to age, housing, behavioral adaptation, air pollution, and access to temperature-controlled environments. These questions are especially relevant in Hong Kong, a densely populated and rapidly aging city with substantial seasonal variation and a growing frequency of hot nights and very hot days.

Earlier Hong Kong evidence emphasized cold-related cardiovascular risk. Using daily data from 2000–2009, Goggins et al. reported that decreases in temperature below an estimated 24°C threshold were associated with increased AMI admissions, while no statistically significant heat effect was observed [1]. The same study identified nitrogen dioxide as an important predictor of AMI admissions.

Hong Kong’s thermal environment has changed since these historical study periods. At the Hong Kong Observatory (HKO), the number of hot nights increased from 10 in 2013 to 61 in 2021, while very hot days increased from 17 to 54 in the same selected-year comparison [2, 4]. Cold days nevertheless continued to occur, indicating that contemporary preparedness must consider potentially overlapping heat and cold hazards.

Demographic change may further alter the distribution of cardiovascular burden. Age-sex-specific population denominators and appropriate person-time offsets are necessary to distinguish changing population structure from changes in admission rates. The environmental context has also evolved: temperature and pollutants should not be treated as statistically interchangeable covariates, and ozone in particular may act as a confounder, effect modifier, or pathway variable.

This study will examine monthly AMI, ischemic stroke, and hemorrhagic stroke admissions among Hong Kong residents aged 35 years or older from 2013 to 2023. It will assess associations with HKO-defined thermal extremes, emphasizing hot nights and cold days, while accounting for population denominators, seasonality, long-term trends, and COVID-period disruption. The objective is not to assume that heat has replaced cold, but to determine whether the historically cold-dominant pattern remains sufficient to describe contemporary cardiovascular hospital burden in a warming and aging Hong Kong.

Weather and pollutants data

Meteorological data was obtained from the HKO. The variables included: daily temperature (mean, minimum, maximum), daily relative humidity, daily total rainfall, the number of hot nights (Tmin ≥ 28°C), the number of very hot days (Tmax ≥ 33°C), the number of extremely hot days (Tmax ≥ 35°C), and the number of cold days (Tmin ≤ 12°C). Daily mean pollutant levels for nitrogen dioxide (NO2), sulfur dioxide (SO2), and ozone (O3) were obtained from the Hong Kong Environmental Protection Department (EPD).
