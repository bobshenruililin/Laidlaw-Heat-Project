# The River Has a Temperature

Hong Kong is supposed to be a city at the mouth of a river. For most of this project it has been a garden in Tsim Sha Tsui.

One thermometer. One set of official flags. One time series that can be matched, month by month, to a hospital file. That is still the honest grain of the paper.

Then the public grid got greedy.

First it compared Hong Kong to Singapore and Phoenix, and the 28°C night refused to travel. Then it looked inside the territory, and Waglan Island lit up while Headquarters stayed dim. This afternoon it painted the water between Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Guangzhou.

On the same 4,017 days, on the same ERA5-Land source, the marine cells south of the estuary collect a few hundred nights with `Tmin ≥ 28°C`. The Guangzhou box collects about twelve. Huizhou, inland and a little higher, collects almost none. Macau’s coordinate, sitting on the estuary, collects 143. Headquarters’ coordinate collects 17.

A 28°C night, on this grid, is not a Guangdong event. It is a marine one.

That sentence is not a health finding. Stroke was not delivered. The CHD/HF file has no addresses, and it has no Shenzhen. Painting first-event counts onto a delta would be a new kind of fiction. The live paper should keep the station.

What the mosaic is allowed to say is smaller, and I think more useful for a Laidlaw website: *Hong Kong* is a place in a field. The field has a north. It has a sea. Neighbours still matter after you have accounted for latitude and elevation — Moran’s I stays positive; a spatial lag of about 0.45 remains. Climate only half-follows the map.

There is now a page for it, with a month slider, a dashed line from Waglan to Guangzhou, and the same cream paper as the other public companions. The banner still will not let you forget the boundary.

I will not post the map until I have read it once more for voice and privacy. The science of the paper does not move. The river just got a temperature.
