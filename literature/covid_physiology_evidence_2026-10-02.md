# Bounded COVID physiology evidence check — 2 October 2026

**Mode:** Explore; primary-source verification and approach-family discrimination. **Provenance:** REAL published external studies; no new local transfer or approved output. This memo contains no estimates from our series, manuscript edits, or new analysis pipeline. Seven primary papers suffice for this bounded search; it is not exhaustive.

**Question:** Could physiological or clinical improvement during/after the pandemic contribute to changing Hong Kong monthly first-hospitalisation counts after first CHD/HF diagnosis among people with T2D/HTN? Admission cause, risk-set denominator and measured physiological trajectories are unavailable in the public receipt. External evidence can establish plausibility, not identify the explanation in these counts.

## Five discriminating physiological/clinical studies

### 1. Local glycaemia and BP: overall null

Wong CM, Lai KPL, Luk MHM, et al. (2025). *Impact of COVID-19 pandemic on glycaemic and blood pressure control among patients with type 2 diabetes in primary care in Hong Kong.* BMC Primary Care 26:182. [DOI 10.1186/s12875-025-02893-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12875-025-02893-z).

Retrospective cross-sectional samples from eight public clinics: 1,605 and 1,507 regular T2D attendees. **Actual Methods dates are 11 February–10 March 2020 and 11 February–10 March 2021**, despite confusing “2019”/“2020” group labels. Baseline already follows the first local outbreak. Adjusted differences: HbA1c +0.019 percentage points (95% CI −0.057 to +0.094), SBP −0.143 mmHg (−1.005 to +0.719), DBP +0.148 mmHg (−0.422 to +0.718). All were null. Female subgroup improvement was small and exploratory. Separate samples and exclusion of care defaulters limit attribution. Closest local evidence supports stability among attendees, not broad improvement or its effect on hospitalisations.

### 2. Taiwan glycaemia: selective improvement

Huang H, Su HL, Huang CH, Lin YH (2023). *Retrospective Study on the Impact of COVID-19 Lockdown on Patients with Type 2 Diabetes in Northern Taiwan.* Diabetes, Metabolic Syndrome and Obesity 16:2539–2547. [DOI 10.2147/DMSO.S422617](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10461745/).

Single-centre retrospective follow-up of 943 treated T2D outpatients: February–May 2021 versus August–October 2021 around May–July restrictions. HbA1c fell 7.0±1.0% to 6.8±0.9%; fasting glucose fell 132.2±33.3 to 124.4±30.0 mg/dL (both p<0.001). BP, lipids, weight/BMI and kidney measurements did not significantly change. Retained-care selection, seasonally different windows, no contemporaneous control, and unavailable lifestyle/medication-change information prevent a lockdown-effect claim. Measured glycaemic improvement is plausible in selected outpatients; broad cardiovascular physiological improvement and Hong Kong count effects remain unproved.

### 3. Japan glycaemia/lipids: small adverse changes

Ohkuma K, Sawada M, Aihara M, et al. (2023). *Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the glycemic control in people with diabetes mellitus: A retrospective cohort study.* Journal of Diabetes Investigation 14:985–993. [DOI 10.1111/jdi.14021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jdi.14021).

University-hospital cohort: 4,247 diabetes patients, predominantly T2D; April 2019–March 2020 versus April 2020–March 2021, including corresponding quarters. T2D annual HbA1c rose 7.05% to 7.11%; total cholesterol and triglycerides also increased slightly. Continued attendance and annual testing were required; COVID hospitalisations were excluded. Prescribing changed; dosages, lifestyle and several clinical covariates were unavailable. Avoid a precise T2D subgroup count because the table heading conflicts with other reported counts. This contradicts universal improvement, but does not estimate the pandemic effect or our hospitalisation pathway.

### 4. Hong Kong infection recovery: adverse/null physiology

Lui DTW, Lee CH, Wong Y, et al. (2024). *A Prospective 1-Year Follow-up of Glycemic Status and C-Peptide Levels of COVID-19 Survivors with Dysglycemia in Acute COVID-19 Infection.* Diabetes & Metabolism Journal 48:763–770. [DOI 10.4093/dmj.2023.0175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11307110/).

Fifty-two survivors with acute dysglycaemia recruited September–October 2020; established diabetes excluded. OGTT, HbA1c, insulin and C-peptide assessed six weeks after infection; 43 returned at one year. HbA1c rose 5.5±0.3% to 5.7±0.2%; 10/42 initially non-diabetic participants progressed in glycaemic category. C-peptide was unchanged; insulin sensitivity decreased. Controls had no repeat assessment. Selected survivors differ from our T2D/HTN population, but local measured recovery evidence does not support uniformly improved glycaemia.

### 5. Vaccination: conditional clinical protection

Wan EYF, Mok AHY, Yan VKC, et al. (2023). *Association between BNT162b2 and CoronaVac vaccination and risk of CVD and mortality after COVID-19 infection: A population-based cohort study.* Cell Reports Medicine 4:101195. [DOI 10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101195](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10591029/).

Hong Kong adult infections, 23 February 2021–31 October 2022; follow-up through 23 January 2023; prior CVD excluded. IPTW-weighted three-dose versus unvaccinated composite CVD HRs: acute 0.42 (95% CI 0.35–0.51), post-acute 0.59 (0.54–0.66). Outcomes were primary-diagnosis CVD hospitalisations, not BP/glycaemia or our admission construct. Residual healthy-vaccinee, socioeconomic and lifestyle confounding remains. Supports vaccination-associated protection conditional on infection; cannot explain an initial 2020 change or establish better population health than pre-COVID.

## Separate utilisation and infection-suppression families

**Lui DTW et al. (2020),** *A territory-wide study on the impact of COVID-19 on diabetes-related acute care*, J Diabetes Investig 11:1303–1306; [DOI 10.1111/jdi.13368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7404850/). HA adults, 25 January–24 April 2020 versus inter-/intra-year controls: fewer severe dysglycaemia admissions, but higher glucose/HbA1c among severe-hyperglycaemia admissions; DKA admissions did not significantly decline. Retrospective coding and selection into hospital limit inference. Demonstrates that fewer admissions can coexist with worse physiology among those admitted.

**Cowling BJ, Ali ST, Ng TWY, et al. (2020),** *Impact assessment of non-pharmaceutical interventions against coronavirus disease 2019 and influenza in Hong Kong: an observational study*, Lancet Public Health 5:e279–e288; [DOI 10.1016/S2468-2667(20)30090-6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7164922/). Early-2020 outpatient influenza surveillance, paediatric admissions and behavioural surveys supported reduced influenza transmission around bundled interventions. Ecological timing, concurrent changes and paediatric endpoints limit attribution. Lower circulating infection exposure is plausible; co-infection or cardiovascular triggers were not measured in our series.

## What survives

Selective outpatient glycaemic improvement remains possible alongside null/adverse evidence. BP improvement has no broad local support here. Restrictions/behaviour, infection suppression, vaccination and infection recovery are distinct mechanisms; none establishes improved population health. Existing counts cannot distinguish them from changed presentation or risk-set composition. Roro owns the missing admission semantics and clinical/risk-set information; no external coefficient can fill those gaps. Stop after these discriminating studies rather than expanding a confirmation search.
