# Laidlaw blog

## Trigger phrases

- `/laidlaw-blog`
- “Laidlaw blog.”
- “MIT Admissions register.”

## Preconditions

- [ ] The week, source material, and intended story are identified.
- [ ] Published context, project plans, environmental descriptives, and real findings can be separated.
- [ ] Any personal moment, image, discomfort, or anticipation shift comes from Bob or the record.

## Execution

1. Read [`knowledge/2026-07-26_laidlaw_blog_canon.md`](../../../knowledge/2026-07-26_laidlaw_blog_canon.md) and [`reports/blog/STYLE_NOTES.md`](../../../reports/blog/STYLE_NOTES.md) first.
2. If the story choice is unclear, ask Bob what moment or change felt most true.
3. Write in Bob’s first person with short paragraphs, one plain technical idea at a time, natural collaborator credit, and an honest week-level arc.
4. Keep published evidence, exposure-only summaries, plans, and project findings explicitly distinct.
5. Save a ready-to-edit draft under `reports/blog/` and update its README index.
6. Stop before publication and ask Bob for a creative, privacy, length, and voice pass.

## Done when

- The post is specific, warm, lightly odd, and intelligible to a general reader.
- The voice is not corporate, motivational, manufactured-quirky, or CNS/manuscript prose.
- Every biographical and scientific detail is supported.
- Bob’s final edit and publication remain pending.

## Claim boundaries

Never invent biography, dialogue, feelings attributed to others, HA fields, coefficients, or findings. Monthly stroke aggregates are not daily mortality or daily DLNM evidence. Synthetic output is never a result.

- `reports/blog/<draft>.md`
- `reports/blog/README.md`
- `analysis_plan/context_compound_log.md` when the draft records a new project arc
