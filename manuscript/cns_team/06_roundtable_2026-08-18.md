## Fable 5

**Verdict.** Unusually honest, numerically verified exploratory ecological report whose prose discipline exceeds its evidential content. It survives referee scrutiny only because it claims almost nothing.

**Caliber 8 / strength 5.** Execution of the panel, ladder, sensitivities, and frozen daily-recovery refusal would be a 7; the data support a 5. One-station exposure; no admission cause, denominators, or age–sex strata; cold-day contrast from 29 of 132 months; CHD lag-1 residual autocorrelation 0.508 (mean-model misspecification, not only an SE problem); no contrast survives multiplicity. HF cold days are the one residual worth carrying; CHD hot nights are an artifact-risk case study.

**Genre.** Calibration-and-identification report on governed monthly aggregates. Not an effect-estimation paper.

**Excellent.** Uncertainty ladder (NW narrowing named as caution); Figure 2 cold-day decomposition; daily-recovery refusal scoped to this series; Figure 1 depletion sentence.

**Fatal to the *stated* claim.** Nothing, because the claim is minimal. Fatal to *submission in present form:* outcome definition still provisional (“should replace this paragraph”); any Abstract reading of 1.022 as a finding dies at *q* = 0.192.

**Major, not paste-blocking.** (a) Confounding deferred where a days-offset NO₂-adjusted core could be fitted. (b) CHD AR structure untreated. (c) Pre-2020 panel is broadly cold-dominant (CHD mean temperature 0.980, *p* = 1.9 × 10⁻⁴; HF 0.945, *p* ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁹; HF hot nights invert to 0.965) while Discussion narrates window-dependence only for hot nights. Figure 3 already displays this. (d) Single-station exposure.

**Journals.** ER: major revision. EHP: reject. IJE: reject as original research; possible methods/education recast. LPH: reject. Nature Communications: reject.

**For Bob.** Ship-as-paste. Hold ICD confirmation, a comparable pollution adjustment, one pre-2020 cold-dominance sentence, and unused refs [3]/[5] for the submission cycle.

**Open questions.** Does HF cold days survive influenza in the *same* core specification? Can any monthly CHD model separate hot nights from 2020–2023 utilisation? Can person-time convert count ratios to incidence?

## Opus 5

**Verdict.** Prose is disciplined enough to survive Hogan and mostly honest enough to survive a methods editor, but the Conclusion makes a comparative claim the paper never tests, and Table 3 is displayed asymmetrically toward the two “leading” contrasts.

**Caliber 8 / strength 6.** Writing more mature than the evidence. NW6 as core at *T* = 132 yields intervals narrower than model-based for CHD hot nights — the signature of a downward-biased HAC variance. A methods editor will ask why the headline is not model-based with HAC as sensitivity.

**CNS checklist (skill).** Pass: opening estimand; sourced numerals; mortality ≠ morbidity; limitations match design; Results distinguish estimates / CIs / prevalence / diagnostics. Mixed: one-claim-per-sentence (hot-night counts recur); citations ([3]/[5] unused; HKO numerals cite [2,4] then [6]); nulls not hidden in Table 2 but ladder shown only for two contrasts; Conclusion exceeds provenance; Hogan register broken by placeholders. Fail: collaborator credit (weather co-investigator thanked anonymously while others are named); draft markers / process in the body.

**Overclaim (quote).** Conclusion: “CHD first hospitalisations were more closely associated with hot nights, and HF first hospitalisations with cold days, than with the other thermal encodings examined.” No encoding-versus-encoding or CHD-versus-HF interaction is fitted. Abstract Conclusions is the correct version.

**Ladder hole (verified).** HF mean temperature model-based 0.974 (0.956–0.993); HF Tmin model-based 0.973 (0.956–0.991). The paper calls continuous HF temperature “weakly inverse” and withholds that ladder.

**Other register breaks.** Author 2–4; “should replace this paragraph”; IRB-amendment sentence; Strengths after Conclusion; “Roadside stations were reserved.”; Hogan/Bob seam at the weather paragraph (verbatim by design).

**Excellent.** Refusals written as findings; Guo paragraph; depletion sentence; “whether a given January or July differs from a typical January or July.”

**Journals.** ER: prose survives desk; science becomes a referee problem. EHP: likely desk-reject. IJE: best conceptual fit if recast.

**For Bob.** Revise-then-paste, five sentences: rewrite Conclusion comparative; extend Table 3 or drop “leading”; delete the co-author instruction in Health data; cut the IRB-amendment sentence; move Strengths above Conclusion and drop the two virtue-contrast clauses.

**Open questions.** Is NW6 defensible as core? Is ACF 0.508 thermal structure or underfitted depletion? Should title/Abstract/Conclusion match the Discussion’s identification-limits frame?
