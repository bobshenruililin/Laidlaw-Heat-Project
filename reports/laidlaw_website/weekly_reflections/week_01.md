# Week 1 reflection — Groundwork before the visible week

When Ernest Hemingway was asked how he went bankrupt, he said it happened “gradually and then suddenly.” I keep meeting that line in a more hopeful form: leadership and research competence also arrive gradually, then all at once — but only if the quiet weeks are allowed to count.

Week 1 of this Laidlaw placement did not feel like a highlight reel. It felt like laying bricks. I read older Hong Kong baselines — especially the Goggins work Hogan had already flagged as the intellectual starting point — and tried to understand why cold associations for stroke and heart attack are already well documented here. Novelty, if there is any for a later period, has to be careful. I also began assembling the environmental side of the study: monthly temperature structure, official extreme-day counts, and the spell / combined day–night ideas that sit in the Ren–Wang tradition the lab already respects.

As someone who likes tangible output, that pace was uncomfortable. There were no stroke coefficients to show. There was a lot of listening, note-taking, and resisting the temptation to invent a cleaner story than the data permissions allow. The general Hospital Authority extract available to us does not carry admission reasons in the way an AMI-and-stroke principal-diagnosis panel would need. That is not a small inconvenience; it is a redesign. Stroke aggregates become the honest near-term outcome. AMI from that file goes out of scope.

What I learned most clearly in Week 1 is a Laidlaw lesson as much as a methods lesson: **value is not equal to output**. Preparing climate layers, writing down assumptions, and naming what we cannot claim yet is the work. Jumping straight to “findings” would have looked busier and been scientifically emptier. The gradual building blocks — reading, framing, plumbing, credit — are what make a sudden later analysis legitimate rather than theatrical.

I am still early. But I trust the plan I am building with my supervisor and collaborators for a reason. The slow fortnight is not a failure of ambition. It is how ambition survives contact with real data constraints.
