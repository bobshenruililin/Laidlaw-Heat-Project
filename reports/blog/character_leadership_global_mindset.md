The Foundation does not ask for another methods appendix. It asks whether the Scholarship left a person who can still tell the truth when a file is late, a coefficient is noisy, and a map is prettier than the estimand.

I am trying to answer in their language.

## Capacities — People

The work this summer was never a solo coding sprint. Weather belongs to Hogan. The hospital file belongs to Zhenyuan Liu. The concept of a multi-method panel, and the permission to wait, belong to Professor Bishai.

That is not courtesy. It is the People list on the 3 C’s page: communication, a collaborative mindset, and something like global citizenship without leaving the city. Hogan writes the weather paragraph; I do not overwrite it with repository prose. Roro constructs the outcome; I do not invent a stroke series because the email named one. Bishai owns Gate 3; an agent cannot freeze a headline.

Diversity and equity, on this project, showed up as a refusal. The CHD/HF file has no addresses. Painting first-event counts onto a Pearl River Delta mosaic would look like a health-equity map and would be a new fiction. The people who live north of the harbour are not in our numerator.

## Capacities — Process

Project management, here, meant keeping one panel instead of ten press releases. Pathway IDs. An assumption ledger. Provenance labels: `REAL`, `SYNTHETIC`, `HA_APPROVED_AGGREGATE`, `REAL_PUBLIC`. When the public climate grid got greedy — sixteen cities, then a Hong Kong lattice, then a delta mosaic — the process rule did not change. Exposure may travel. Hospital ratios may not.

Prioritisation was unromantic. The Foundation wants the essay on the Network by 31 August. The poster file is due 15 September. The locked PDFs already exist; rebuilding them for word-count optics would be the wrong kind of fast. The live manuscript still needs a human paste. Stroke is still missing. Those are the actual bottlenecks. Maps are what you can honestly build while you wait.

Impact measurement, for us, is not a dashboard of relative risks. It is whether the next session inherits a sharper question. That is a Laidlaw-shaped metric, and it is also just good lab hygiene.

## Capacities — Performance

Critical thinking showed up as a sequence of no’s. No AMI from a file without admission reasons. No daily DLNM on 132 monthly counts. No transporting Hong Kong hospitalisation ratios to Guangzhou because the nights are cooler there on ERA5. No quoting Open-Meteo as an HKO yearbook.

Creative ideation is allowed. A 0.2° mosaic, an EOF of night temperature, a Theil–Sen trend — those are ideas. They become findings only if we forget the banner on the page. I am trying not to forget it.

Strategy is the Gate 3 recommendation sitting open: an exploratory paper, not a confirmatory primary. That is a performance choice. It is also Character.

## Character

The Character list is long. A few of the words did real work.

**Integrity** is the provenance label. If the night count is ERA5, the sentence says ERA5. If the event is first hospitalisation after first CHD or HF diagnosis, the sentence does not say heart attack.

**Humility** is leaving Professor Bishai’s comment box empty. An agent can draft a worksheet. It cannot circle *satisfactory*.

**Judgment** is treating synthetic dry-runs as plumbing, not as results.

**Accountability** is writing the meeting into the ledger so the next person does not re-walk a dead end.

**Courage**, on a research summer, is smaller than the word. It is sending a supervisor an essay that still says all twelve core *q*-values exceed 0.19, instead of shopping for the interval that excludes one.

**Collaboration** and **humanity** are the same instruction in this lab: go far together, and do not steal someone else’s paragraph to look finished.

I am less sure about transcendence and temperance. Temperance might be not adding another map the night before the form is due. Transcendence might be remembering that a Scholar Network post is for a wide room, not for a referee.

## Change Maker Values

The official values are ambitious, brave, curious, determined, extraordinary, fast, and good.

Curious is easy. The public grid will answer almost any spatial question you ask it.

Determined is waiting for a stroke file that was named and not attached.

Fast is a vice unless it is the 31 August deadline. The Foundation also wants *good*. Good, here, is not a larger coefficient. It is a sentence that will still be true in October, when someone who is not me reads the poster in Chi Wah.

Brave is publishing the null-leaning panel rather than a story. Extraordinary is not a word I would claim for monthly counts. Ambitious is wanting the live paper to survive Hogan’s weather lock *and* a Dean’s reading of the Network essay. Those are different audiences. They are allowed to remain different.

## Global mindset

A global mindset is not a boarding pass. It is noticing that Hong Kong is not Singapore on the same temperature grid, that a 28°C night on ERA5 is a marine object in the Pearl, and that a method borrowed from climate dynamics (an EOF) does not license a health claim in Guangdong.

It is also the Network itself: the University room, the badges, the URL Horizons will later send the Foundation. A GitHub map can sit beside that post. It cannot replace it.

I will keep acting as a global citizen by refusing to export other people’s hospitalisations, and by keeping the public climate work labelled as climate. Ethical leadership, on this project, is mostly that refusal plus credit: Hogan’s weather, Roro’s file, Bishai’s gate.

The 2027 Leadership in Action placement is a different assignment. This summer’s job was to learn how not to become a person who needs a finding every week.
