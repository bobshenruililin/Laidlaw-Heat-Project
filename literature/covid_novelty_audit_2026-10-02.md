# COVID cardiovascular manuscript: bounded novelty audit

**Audit date:** 2 October 2026. **Provenance:** published external research and the existing `HA_APPROVED_AGGREGATE` manuscript/receipt; no new clinical extract. This is a targeted competitor audit, not a systematic review or proof that an unanswered question is globally unique. No outcome-specific population incidence or physiological benefit is inferred from our counts.

## Decision

The broad question of falling cardiovascular records during pandemic care disruption is already occupied. Hong Kong studies now cover hypertension, diabetes, clinical control, mortality, care utilisation and later complications. Merely adding laboratory covariates, extending follow-up to 2023, or plotting weather coefficients will not establish a leading-family contribution. The strongest candidate is a **measurement-and-mechanism discrimination study**: determine whether apparent first-event reductions survive harmonised event definitions and eligible person-time, then compare independently specified predictions of disease change, altered care contact and ascertainment. This is a proposed contribution, not demonstrated novelty or identification.

## Closest studies and checked boundaries

### N01 — Youn et al., 2025: diabetes, Hong Kong and Korea

[Youn HM, Hu Z, Park YS, et al. Health Science Reports 8:e71291. DOI 10.1002/hsr2.71291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41049877/).

**Status: abstract checked; full text and supplement not checked.** PMC presented a browser challenge; Wiley and Europe PMC did not return usable full text. Do not treat search-index snippets as a complete Methods audit.

The abstract describes a closed diabetes cohort established in 2014, followed in 2015–2021 without prior CVD or recorded SARS-CoV-2 infection, and monthly interrupted time-series analyses of mortality, CVD incidence and service use. Hong Kong CVD records fell in 2020 and recovered in 2021. Exact event codes, eligible denominator construction and infection-selection implementation remain unresolved. Two abstract inconsistencies require checking: the stated total differs from the sum of country counts; the Korea 2021 point estimate lies outside its printed interval. Neither is imported into our manuscript.

**Novelty implication:** diabetes plus another population plus service-use trajectories is already a published design; our diagnosis-associated hospital stay is not automatically the same outcome as its CVD incidence.

### N02 — Hu et al., 2025: hypertension through early Omicron

[Hu Z, Yau YK, Quan J, et al. Hypertension Research 48:2197–2208. DOI 10.1038/s41440-025-02230-y](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41440-025-02230-y). [Author-repository abstract](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/indirect-effect-of-thecovid-19-pandemic-on-cardiovascular-disease/).

**Status: abstract checked; full text and supplement not checked.** The publisher fetch failed; the openly listed accepted-manuscript PDF returned 403. Access controls were not bypassed.

The study follows hypertension patients until death, recorded SARS-CoV infection or April 2022, with pre-pandemic, early-pandemic, interwave and Omicron periods. It examines CVD incidence, in/out-of-hospital mortality, office BP control and care use. The abstract reports an initial CVD decline followed by recovery above the prior level, and worse BP-control proportions. Code lists, risk-set denominators, BP measurement selection and full identification assumptions need the Methods/supplement.

**Novelty implication:** hypertension, BP and Omicron timing are not unoccupied territory. Infection censoring changes the target population; it does not by itself identify an indirect pandemic effect.

### N03 — Yau et al., 2026: continuity of care and later cardiovascular outcomes

[Yau YK, Li M, Quan J, et al. Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 28:8016–8024. DOI 10.1111/dom.70984](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13449042/).

**Status: full main text checked; supplement contents not checked.** The two linked Word supplements did not return usable contents. Locators: Methods 2.1–2.5; Discussion limitations; supplementary-file catalogue.

The June 2022 landmark study compares clinic continuity changes in August 2017–December 2019 versus January 2020–May 2022, following incident CHD/HF/stroke, kidney failure and death through June 2024. Selection requires at least three visits in both windows, survival and absence of CVD/kidney failure at baseline; increased continuity and recorded COVID through follow-up are excluded. Clinical covariates include HbA1c, BP, LDL and renal function. Codes reside in unchecked Table S1. Cox analyses include death competing-risk and infection-inclusion sensitivities. The authors acknowledge survivor selection and inability to separate patient behaviour from system reallocation.

**Novelty implication:** continuity, laboratory covariates and later CHD/HF outcomes already coexist in a local paper. Our extension must improve discrimination, measurement or transportability, rather than replicate its feature list.

### N04 — Xin et al., 2023: population hospitalisations and deaths

[Xin H, Wu P, Wong JY, et al. Lancet Regional Health – Western Pacific 30:100645. DOI 10.1016/j.lanwpc.2022.100645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36438907/).

**Status: abstract checked this run; no new full-text or supplement audit.** The current manuscript already used this study; PMC returned a browser challenge in this audit.

Weekly 2010–2020 interrupted time-series models compare 2020 with a prior trend, considering cause-specific hospitalisations and in/out-of-hospital deaths. Fewer hospitalisations accompanied more deaths. This is a population burden comparison, not our eligible first-event denominator or clinical cohort. Exact coding and population-standardisation details remain outside this run's verification.

**Novelty implication:** admissions down/mortality up is established local context, not our proposed discovery. Trend extrapolation requires counterfactual stability and cannot uniquely attribute a bundle of simultaneous changes.

### N05 — Wong et al., 2025: measured HbA1c and BP among regular attenders

[Wong CM, Lai KPL, Luk MHM, et al. BMC Primary Care 26:182. DOI 10.1186/s12875-025-02893-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12875-025-02893-z).

**Status: full main text checked; no separate supplement used.** Locators: Methods/Subject and Data collection; multivariate Results. Despite group labels of 2019/2020, the actual windows are 11 February–10 March **2020** and **2021**. These are separate samples of regularly followed T2D patients tested for HbA1c; recent diabetes diagnoses and irregular attenders are excluded. BP, complications and treatment are recorded. No overall adjusted improvement is supported, but absence of statistical evidence is not an equivalence result. Baseline already follows the initial local outbreak.

**Novelty implication:** aggregate clinical improvement must be assessed with the testing/attendance process and a genuine pre-outbreak window. These selected samples neither identify population physiology nor validate our admission mechanism.

### N06 — Cezard et al., 2024: independent postinfection evidence

[Cezard GI, Denholm RE, Knight R, et al. Nature Communications 15:2173. DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-46497-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-46497-0).

**Status: full main text checked; supplementary contents and code not audited.** Locators: Methods/Study population and Statistical analyses; Discussion limitations. Linked English primary-care, hospital, infection, vaccination and death records support outcome-specific first-event follow-up and person-years. Infection timing is time varying; calendar-time Cox models separate day-zero and later periods, with vaccination/variant cohorts. Cardiovascular associations after diagnosis are weaker among vaccinated people. The paper notes event-driven infection detection, changing testing and unmeasured/time-varying confounding. Clinical phenotypes combine record sources and any-position codes, with primary-position sensitivity.

**Novelty implication:** evidence that infection raises cardiovascular risk can coexist with falling recorded admissions. This is independent contextual evidence, not replication of our first post-diagnosis stay or a transportable coefficient.

### N07 — Spetz et al., 2025: Swedish linked-register corroboration

[Spetz M, Natt och Dag Y, Li H, et al. Nature Communications 16:10115. DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-66270-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66270-1).

**Status: main-text relevant Methods/Results/Discussion checked; supplements not checked.** National linked registers study ages 40–75, incident cardiovascular outcomes and mortality, with infection timing, vaccination, severity and social factors. Patterns vary across outcome, severity and time since infection. Admission-defined severity can change meaning across pandemic waves.

**Novelty implication:** population postinfection cardiovascular follow-up is also established beyond VA cohorts. This is corroboration of a different estimand, not external validation of the present extract or evidence that all outcomes worsen equally.

## Operational gap and contribution boundary

**Feasible now:** accurately describe existing recorded burden; expose sensitivity of thermal associations; specify an observation-process diagram and rival predictions; reconcile receipt claims with the unavailable query; prepare a minimum governed data request. None resolves the clinical mechanisms.

**Candidate extension requiring new evidence:** contrast the current diagnosis-associated first-stay series with validated cause-coded first cardiovascular events and deaths in a harmonised eligible population. Construct event-free person-time, distinguish risk-set composition from rate changes, and follow clinical control alongside care contacts and probability of being measured. Retain infection histories instead of automatically excluding everyone ever infected. Define the intended target before deciding whether infection is an exposure, censoring event, confounder or selection criterion. Compare a prespecified observation-process prediction in an independently governed cohort.

**What would make this progressive:** a validated measurement contrast reverses or explains the apparent admission improvement; independent predictions distinguish a care/recording change from true risk change; or an adequately severe test rejects the proposed improvement explanation. A null or unresolved contrast can be informative but needs justified precision and discrimination. No decomposition into disease, care and coding effects is identified merely because several series are measured.

**What remains unproved:** global uniqueness; data availability; principal-diagnosis validity; eligible person-time; laboratory representativeness; causal mediation; independent replication feasibility; leading-journal suitability. Retrieve N01/N02 Methods and all decisive supplements before claiming a gap those publications might already cover.
