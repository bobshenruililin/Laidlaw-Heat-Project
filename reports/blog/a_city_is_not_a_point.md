# Hong Kong Is Not a Point

For most of this project, Hong Kong has been a single coordinate.

HKO Headquarters. One thermometer, one set of official flags, one time series that can be matched, month by month, to a hospital file. That is the honest grain of the paper. A territory-month does not become a street.

Then a public reanalysis grid arrived, and the coordinate started to look lonely.

On the same 4,017 days, the official hot-night rule (`Tmin ≥ 28°C`) happens 449 times at Headquarters and 17 times in the ERA5-Land cell that sits on that coordinate. That was last night’s surprise. This morning’s version is more awkward: even *inside* the territory, on that same grid, the flag will not sit still.

Waglan Island, a marine speck south-east of the harbour, records 252 of those ERA5 nights. Tuen Mun records 123. Headquarters records 17. Victoria Peak, after Open-Meteo’s elevation correction, records none. Ta Kwu Ling, in the northern New Territories, collects more cold days than the station we actually use.

This is not a health map. It is not eighteen districts coloured by hospital counts. Stroke was not delivered. The CHD/HF file has no addresses. Painting those counts onto a lattice would be a different kind of fiction from the one we already refused.

It is a climate object with a location.

A two-line spatial model on thirty lattice cells says the obvious thing first: nights get colder as the DEM gets higher, about 0.6°C per hundred metres. Distance-to-coast, on an island this small, does almost nothing. Adding latitude and longitude lifts the fit because the north is simply cooler at night. Moran’s I on the residuals stays positive. The leftover is still a field, not white noise.

Hogan has been asking, in plainer language, whether Headquarters is the weather of Hong Kong or the weather of one garden in Tsim Sha Tsui. The live paper should keep the station. Official flags are station objects. What the lattice adds is a warning we can now point at: exporting those flags across the harbour, let alone to Singapore or Phoenix, is a spatial act.

The Laidlaw site is where this kind of sentence belongs. Not a coefficient. A change in what the word *Hong Kong* is allowed to mean while we wait for the team to freeze a headline.

There is now a map for it, with the same cream paper as the other public pages, and a banner that will not let you forget the boundary:

Exposure only. Not health findings. Not a district health map.

I will not post the map until I have read it once more for voice and privacy. The science of the paper does not move. The geography of the exposure just became visible.
