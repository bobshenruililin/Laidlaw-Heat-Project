# Talking points — Bishai on Figure 3 (27 August 2026)

Use with the teaching figure in this folder. Numbers are `HA_APPROVED_AGGREGATE`. Gate 3 remains open. Form 2a is unchanged.

## One paragraph

The rows that look like 8-, 6-, and 5-day splits are statistical controls — how flexible we let the long-term time trend be — and the “per 5 days” in the title is the reporting unit, five extra official hot nights or cold days in a month. There is no 6-day heatwave in that figure. The CHD hot-night estimate sits near 1.02 once the trend is flexible enough, but it is compatible with 1 with a stiff trend or in 2013–2019, and none of the twelve estimates survives multiplicity control. The interval that sits entirely away from 1 is heart failure and cold days before 2020: 1.113 (1.053–1.176). That is a cold result, not a heat one. A monthly panel cannot test a daily 5-day warning rule. The physiology that would motivate a multi-day night rule is failed overnight recovery, which is a hypothesis. For heat-health planning, night-time heat and winter heart failure deserve attention in existing HKO/DH instruments; no threshold is validated.

## If he asks “what is pre-COVID?”

January 2013–December 2019, 84 months. Not a causal COVID model. CHD hot nights 1.011 (0.991–1.032) **includes 1**. HF cold days 1.113 (1.053–1.176) **excludes 1**.

## If he asks “is there truth to the 5-day rule?”

Three different things: (1) our reporting scale, yes, locked; (2) consecutive duration, not identified here; (3) HKO “Prolonged Heat” is already “a few days” of hot days or hot nights, qualitative. Physiology: consecutive hot nights can deny overnight recovery. We cannot pick 5 versus 3 versus 8 from 132 months.

## Do not say

The figure compares 8/6/5-day heatwaves. This validates a warning trigger. Pre-COVID confirms CHD hot nights. Hong Kong has one named HHAP that we evaluated. Gate 3 is closed.
