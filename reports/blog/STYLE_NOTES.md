# Style notes for future Laidlaw blogs

## Do

- Write in Bob's first person, with short paragraphs and a visible week-level arc.
- Use the MIT Admissions Blogs register: specific, curious, lightly odd, warm, and self-aware.
- Let methods supply the imagery: recipes, thresholds, waiting, revisions, awkward labels.
- Explain one technical idea at a time for a general reader.
- Credit Hogan, Roro/Zhenyuan, Dr Bishai, and others where their contribution moves the story.
- Separate published context, environmental descriptives, planned analysis, and actual project findings.
- Bring Bob into outlining and voice choices earlier in future weeks.

## Don't

- Invent childhood, family, romance, hometown, private dialogue, or other biography.
- Make Bob sound like a university press office, a motivational speaker, or a quirk generator.
- Import CNS/manuscript prose into the blog.
- Turn waiting into fake progress or synthetic output into evidence.
- Claim AMI from the general HA file, daily effects from monthly data, or stroke findings before governed aggregates are analysed.
- Treat every method as a separate discovery.
- Turn a data-arrival week into a coefficient inventory, a *q*-value dump, or a plot twist. Week 5’s job is the name of the file and the work of running what was prepared — not Table 2.
- Do not number a new post as Week 3. That slot is the published hot-month post (`week2_definitions_and_waiting.md`). After that: Week 4 (Methods while waiting), Week 5 (the CHD/HF file), Week 6 (the file did not finish the paper).

Bob makes the final editorial and privacy decisions before anything is posted.
