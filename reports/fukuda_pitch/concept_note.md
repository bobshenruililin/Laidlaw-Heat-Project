## Empirical starting point (already done; exploratory)

Under Professor David Bishai, with weather and health-data co-investigators, I completed a Laidlaw Summer-1 study of monthly thermal encodings and first CHD/HF hospitalisation counts among people with diabetes or hypertension in Hong Kong, 2013–2023 (132 months). Admission cause was not recorded; a stroke series was not delivered. After twelve comparisons, all *q*-values exceeded 0.19. A planned daily-exposure recovery failed calibration; no daily coefficient was admitted. The scientific residue is therefore **identification**: what monthly aggregates can and cannot support, stated in the same document as the estimates.

That object should not be sold as a confirmatory thermal effect. It is useful as a worked example of **honest, insufficient evidence** in a high-capacity Asian health system.

## Question for this brief

When climate–hospitalisation evidence is exploratory, ecological, and missing cause of admission, what should a health-security decision-maker in Hong Kong (and, if you agree, one Greater Bay Area / Mainland comparator) do with it—watch, invest in better data, or change operations?

This is the same class of problem you have already worked at WHO and CDC: how to act under incomplete evidence without pretending the file is stronger than it is (IHR/PHEIC, the Pandemic Influenza Preparedness Framework, SARS and H5N1 work in China, H1N1, AMR diplomacy). I am not asking you to become a climate statistician. I am asking you to supervise the **translation** step.
