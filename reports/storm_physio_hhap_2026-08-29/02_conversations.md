## Conversation A — identification epidemiologist

**Q. What did 132 months identify?**
A. Under Newey–West lag-6 Model 1, CHD first-hospitalisation counts were 1.022 (1.002–1.042) per five additional official hot-night days in a month (*q* = 0.192). HF counts were 1.073 (1.006–1.144) per five additional official cold days (*q* = 0.192). All twelve Model 1 *q*-values exceed 0.19. Pre-2020 CHD hot nights include 1 (1.011, 0.991–1.032). Pre-2020 HF cold days exclude 1 (1.113, 1.053–1.176). Very hot days leave no CHD residual of the same kind (Table 2: 0.999, 0.974–1.025).

**Q. Is five days a consecutive warning?**
A. No. Model 1 divides the monthly official count by five (`I(count/5)`). That is a linear reporting scale. A monthly total does not distinguish consecutive days from days scattered through the month. In 2018 Hong Kong had 26 official hot nights and zero days inside ≥5-night spells (crown physiology packet). Wang et al. 2019 and Ho et al. 2017 are daily *mortality* consecutive-night studies, not this first-event panel.

**Q. Did the panel evaluate VHWW or a heat–health action plan?**
A. No. The scientific body states that Hong Kong issues official heat and cold warnings and that these estimates do not evaluate those warnings.

## Conversation B — cardiovascular / thermal physiologist

**Q. Why might nights matter for CHD more than very hot days?**
A. Sleep onset is coupled to a nighttime fall in core temperature. Chevance et al. (*Sleep Med Rev* 2024;75:101915) review ambient heat and sleep: hotter conditions are associated with shorter and more fragmented sleep. Ioannou et al. (*Appl Physiol Nutr Metab* 2024;49:1394–1408, DOI 10.1139/apnm-2024-0105) confined seven young men for ten days; nights 4–6 at 26.3 °C left nocturnal core temperature 0.2 °C higher during *and after* those nights, and total sleep time fell ~14 min per 0.1 °C rise in overnight core temperature. O’Connor et al. (*BMC Med* 2025;23:703) found bedroom temperatures above 24 °C associated with lower heart-rate variability in 47 adults ≥65 years. Guo et al. 2024 already showed, in Hong Kong daily emergency hospitalisation, that the official hot-night *flag* can be null after mean temperature while hourly nighttime excess heat is associated. None of these studies is this T2D/HTN first-CHD monthly series. The claim is a hypothesis.

**Q. Do hot nights abolish nocturnal blood-pressure dipping here?**
A. Must not say that. Ashe et al. (*Syst Rev* 2025;14:19) found only three extreme-heat–sleep–cardiovascular studies; blood-pressure findings were mixed; none demonstrated sleep as a mediator of hospitalisation. This panel did not measure blood pressure, sleep, or indoor temperature.

**Q. Why might cold days matter for HF?**
A. Ikäheimo (*Temperature* 2018;5:123–146) reviews cold-induced sympathetic vasoconstriction, raised peripheral resistance, and increased cardiac workload. Li et al. (*Front Physiol* 2026;16:1740919) synthesise cold as a sympathomimetic afterload stress with HF decompensation as a clinical risk. Goggins and Chan (*Int J Cardiol* 2017;228:537–542) reported a Hong Kong daily HF admission RR of 2.63 comparing 11 °C with 25 °C — a different estimand from 1.073 per five official cold days. Influenza, indoor conditions, and care-seeking are unseparated on a monthly grain.

## Conversation C — HHAP planner (WHO 2026)

**Q. Which structure should the paper implement?**
A. WHO Regional Office for Europe, *Heat–health action plans: guidance*, 2nd ed., 2026 (ISBN 9789289062930): eight core elements — (1) governance, (2) heat–health warning system, (3) populations at increased risk, (4) communication, (5) health-system resilience, (6) reducing heat exposure, (7) heat–health surveillance, (8) monitoring, evaluation and learning. Do not invent a municipal HHAP that Hong Kong has not published under that name.

**Q. What instruments already exist in Hong Kong?**
A. Chong and Law (HKO educational article, December 2023): Hot Weather Special Advisory; Very Hot Weather Warning; Prolonged Heat Special Alert when VHWW is in force *and* a few days of very hot days *or* hot nights are encountered; Extremely Hot Weather Special Alert near 35 °C; Hong Kong Heat Index at King’s Park (precautions around HKHI ≥ 30). CHP “Beware of Heat Stroke” (11 August 2025): infants, elderly, pregnant women, chronic illness *including heart disease or high blood pressure*, outdoor workers, overweight people; indoor ventilation or air-conditioning. HAD temporary heat shelters. Separate Cold Weather Warning. Labour Heat Stress at Work Warning is part of the bundle and is not evaluated here.
