\sechead{INTRODUCTION}
\body
Hong Kong's hot nights became more frequent over the past decade: at the Hong
Kong Observatory they rose from 10 days in 2013 to a peak of 61 in
2021\textsuperscript{[1]}, while winter cold days have not disappeared. For the
growing number of people living with type 2 diabetes or hypertension --- a group
at high cardiovascular risk --- hospital burden\textsuperscript{[2,3]}
complements the better-studied mortality burden\textsuperscript{[4,5]}. This
study examines the monthly association between temperature, extreme-day burdens
and first hospitalisation after cardiovascular diagnosis in that group.

\vspace{6mm}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_poster_weather.pdf}\par
\vspace{2.5mm}
\begin{flushleft}
\captext{Official Hong Kong Observatory extremes at Headquarters. Hot nights
rose about fivefold over the decade; cold days recurred every winter --- the
exposure setting for this study.}
\end{flushleft}
\end{minipage}\hfill\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\textwidth}
\sechead{OBJECTIVE}
\begin{questionbox}
\fontsize{28}{35.5}\selectfont\color{Navy}
Are monthly temperature and extreme-day burdens associated with
\textbf{first hospitalisation after a recorded coronary heart disease (CHD) or
heart failure (HF) diagnosis} in a high-risk Hong Kong cohort?\par
\end{questionbox}

\end{tikzpicture}\par
\vspace{6mm}
\begin{flushleft}
\body
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Window and weather:} 132 territory-months, Jan 2013 -- Dec 2023;
        daily records and official extreme-day definitions from the Hong Kong
        Observatory.
  \item \textbf{Outcomes:} Hospital Authority monthly counts of \emph{first
        hospitalisation after a first recorded CHD or HF diagnosis} in people
        with type 2 diabetes and/or hypertension --- 156{,}156 CHD and 29{,}681
        HF events. Delivered columns are labelled inpatient; admission cause is
        not recorded, and final event semantics require data-provider
        confirmation.
  \item \textbf{Model 1 (shown):} 2 outcomes \texttimes{} 6 thermal exposures
        = 12 negative-binomial regressions with calendar-month indicators, a
        trend spline, and a days-in-month offset, specified after the outcome
        series was available. \textbf{Model 2} adds monthly humidity and
        rainfall; \textbf{Model 3} counts days above or below the historical
        same-calendar-day mean. Models 2 and 3 are specified, not shown.
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty:} four standard-error methods per contrast;
        Benjamini--Hochberg \textit{q} across all 12 comparisons.
\end{itemize}
\end{flushleft}
\vspace{2mm}
{\color{Ink}\fontsize{20}{25}\selectfont\textbf{Definitions.} Hot night:
minimum 28\,\textdegree C or higher; very hot day: maximum 33\,\textdegree C or
higher; cold day: minimum 12\,\textdegree C or lower (HKO
definitions)\textsuperscript{[1]}. Estimates are count ratios per 5 extreme days
or per 1\,\textdegree C.\par}
\end{minipage}

\body
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unequal coherence:} the HF cold-day interval excludes 1 under all
        four standard-error methods and before 2020; the CHD hot-night interval
        includes 1 under two methods and in 2013--2019.
  \item The other ten contrasts sit near 1. \textbf{All 12 Benjamini--Hochberg
        \textit{q}-values exceed 0.19}: after correction for 12 comparisons,
        both signals remain exploratory.
\end{itemize}

\sechead{INTERPRETATION / LIMITATIONS}
\body
\begin{itemize}
  \item All 12 \textit{q}-values exceed 0.19 --- these are \textbf{exploratory
        signals}, not confirmed effects.
  \item The CHD hot-night estimate is \textbf{window-dependent and
        uncertainty-sensitive}; the HF cold-day estimate is more coherent
        across methods and before 2020, but not confirmed.
  \item Monthly, territory-level data with no recorded admission cause support
        \textbf{no causal or principal-diagnosis claim}\textsuperscript{[6]}.
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}\hfill\begin{minipage}[t]{0.485\linewidth}
\sechead{CONCLUSION}
\begin{takeawaybox}
\fontsize{27}{34.5}\selectfont
Monthly data suggest \textbf{different thermal patterns} in the CHD and HF
series --- hot nights for CHD, cold days for HF --- but the evidence is
\textbf{exploratory, not confirmatory}: no Model 1 contrast survived
correction for twelve comparisons. Models 2--3 are specified, not shown.
The project leaves a \textbf{reproducible monthly framework} and a clear
next requirement: \textbf{daily or cohort data with recorded admission
cause}.\par
\end{takeawaybox}

\vspace{3mm}
\notetext{\textbf{What would strengthen this:} linked daily weather and
admission records with cause of admission, so the monthly burden patterns seen
here can be tested at the resolution at which heat and cold act.}
\end{minipage}%
