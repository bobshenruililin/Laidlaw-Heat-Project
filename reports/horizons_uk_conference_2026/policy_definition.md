## 3. The undefined point (why you must not buy today)

Two rules sit next to each other and do not resolve the present market:

1. **Ceiling language:** “Up to HK$8,000.”
2. **Eligibility language:** “Only the lowest economy airfare will be approved.”

Possible readings, all live until Horizons picks one:

| Reading | What it would mean for a ticket of, say, HK$11,000 | Can you rely on it without a new email? |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Ceiling** | They reimburse HK$8,000; you pay HK$3,000 | No |
| **B. Exception** | They raise the cap to today’s lowest economy | No |
| **C. Hard eligibility** | Ticket > HK$8,000 is not reimbursable at all | No — and this is the dangerous default if you buy first |

Reading C is why you do not purchase on the strength of the July note. If they later treat the cap as an eligibility test, a bought ticket above HK$8,000 can fail the claim in full. Combined with the non-attendance clause, you would then hold an unused or used international ticket with no Horizons payment.

A second trap sits inside “lowest economy.” Even under reading A, they can later say the ticket you bought was not the cheapest comparable economy fare and pay only that cheaper comparator, then cap at HK$8,000. So any approval must name **a specific quote** (airline, dates, total including tax, screenshot date).

A third trap sits in the extra London days. HKU units commonly reimburse the **lesser of** (actual ticket) and (cheapest economy on the official activity dates). Horizons already allowed 15/23 October when it was cheaper. If 15/23 October is now **more** expensive than 15–18 or 15–19 October, they can restrict reimbursement to the cheaper conference-feasible fare. Buy the cheapest fare that still covers the conference, not the most convenient one.

## 4. HKU-typical practice (background only)

This is **not** Horizons’ written rule for this conference. It is how other HKU travel schemes usually treat economy airfare, so you know what they might say. Do not treat it as a promise.

Typical pattern in HKU faculty/FEO notes:

- cheapest **economy** on the **most direct** route, including tax;
- if you lengthen the trip, supply a quote for the cheapest economy on the **official activity dates**; they pay the lesser of actual cost and that quote;
- a stated “up to $X” is usually `min(actual, cheapest comparable, cap)`.

Under that pattern, a ticket above HK$8,000 would often still be claimable **up to the cap**, with the scholar paying the rest — **if** it was the cheapest eligible economy fare. Horizons has not said they will follow that pattern here. Ask them.

Sources for that background, not for this claim: Faculty of Arts reimbursement checklist (deviation from approved dates requires a same-dates cheapest-economy quote; FEO pays the lesser); Faculty of Arts FGEF; Faculty of Science conference sponsorship (cheapest return economy, most direct route, location caps).
