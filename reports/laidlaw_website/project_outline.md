# Project Outline — Researching at HKU

**Title:** Monthly thermal extremes and stroke hospital burden in Hong Kong, 2013–2023

**Supervisor:** Professor David Bishai  

**Lab / School:** Bishai Lab · School of Public Health, The University of Hong Kong  

**Programme:** Laidlaw Scholars Programme

---

## Background to the Project

Hong Kong’s climate is changing in ways that are already visible in official weather records: hot nights and very hot days have become more common in recent years, while cold days have not simply vanished. At the same time, an ageing population means more people live with the kind of vulnerability that makes extreme temperature medically important.

Stroke is a leading cause of death and disability. Older Hong Kong studies (notably Goggins and colleagues) already showed that **cold** weather can raise hospitalisations for stroke and heart attack. Heat is harder to tell a clean story about — partly because “a heatwave” is not one thing. Different scientific rules flag different months as extreme. Lab work on heatwave–**mortality** (deaths) is complementary context; this project asks a different morbidity question about **stroke burden**.

Hospital Authority data available for this project support **stroke aggregates** (monthly counts), not a full diagnosis-by-diagnosis panel from a general extract that lacks admission reasons. That constraint reshaped the aim: stay precise about what the data can answer.

---

## My aim

My aim is to estimate how **monthly** thermal exposures in Hong Kong from January 2013 to December 2023 associate with **stroke event aggregates**, and to test how stable that picture is across a shared, labelled panel of heat, cold, spell, and sensitivity specifications.

I am deliberately *not* running a day-by-day DLNM admissions study, and I am not claiming individual causality or inventing coefficients while outcome files are still in governance.

I hope the work contributes a careful, reproducible piece to Hong Kong’s climate–health evidence — one that respects collaborators’ scientific leads and refuses to dress readiness up as results.

---

## Objectives

**Main objective:** build and (once governed stroke aggregates arrive) analyse a monthly climate–health panel linking temperature and defined extremes to stroke burden for 132 months (2013–2023).

To do this I will:

1. Assemble public environmental layers — HKO temperature and official extremes, Ren/Wang-style heat **spells** and combined day–night events (**2D3N**), EPD pollution, population denominators, and CHP influenza where complete.
2. Explore a **labelled multi-specification panel** (continuous temperature and lags; official extreme-day counts; spells / 2D3N; alternative heatwave-month definitions; cold and flu co-exposure; age structure; staged pollution and other sensitivities) — one shared question, many named checks, not seventeen separate “discoveries.”
3. Lock weather / heatwave / hot-month operational definitions **with** Hogan, not around him.
4. Merge stroke aggregates once Roro’s timing rule, dictionary, and transfer path are confirmed (first GOPC stroke mention as a marker for an earlier true event month).
5. Freeze headline models with the team before primary claims, and write Stage 3 essay / poster materials that stay honest about what is ready.

**To measure and communicate results, I plan to:**

- Report rate ratios from count models with clear provenance labels (`REAL` vs practice runs).
- Use descriptive climate and pollution figures now; reserve health-effect claims until real outcomes are analysed under governance.
- Produce a literature-and-methods essay and an A0 portrait poster for Laidlaw Stage 3.

Questions, challenges, and corrections are welcome — especially from anyone working on heat, cold, air pollution, or cerebrovascular outcomes in Hong Kong.
