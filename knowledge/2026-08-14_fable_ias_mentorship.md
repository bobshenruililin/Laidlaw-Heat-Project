## 1. The research insight

The maps produced one insight, and it is not "we made maps."

EOF1 of de-seasoned Tmin holds 92% of the variance across the Pearl River Delta grid. For monthly means the basin moves as one pulse, so a well-sited station is a sufficient instrument: ERA5-Land at the Observatory coordinate tracks the station's monthly mean Tmin at r = 0.986.

The official 28°C hot-night flag behaves in the opposite way. Daily agreement between station and reanalysis at that threshold is Jaccard 0.031; the best achievable overlap against the fixed HKO set is 0.47, at 26.3°C; and the count-matching threshold of 26.4°C selects 448 different nights. The flag is a property of one instrument at one coordinate. It does not export.

The paper uses both encodings in the same twelve-contrast panel. Its two exposure families therefore have opposite portability, and the passion projects measured that asymmetry instead of assuming it. That is the contribution: the mean exposures are corroborated by an independent gridded source, while the hot-night exposure rests on the Headquarters record alone — and Headquarters was the only record that could carry the official definition.

None of this moves a health coefficient. All twelve core q-values still exceed 0.19. The maps explain why a null-ish hot-night interval is unsurprising (28.8% identifying share, six calendar months); they do not rescue it.

Overnight measurement of that residual (script 57): June–September has at least one hot night in every one of the eleven years. Within core summer, identification is intensive — how many nights — never whether any occurred. July 2013 had 1 night; July 2022 had 25. May fails twice, October seven times. Cold days have no always-on winter month.
