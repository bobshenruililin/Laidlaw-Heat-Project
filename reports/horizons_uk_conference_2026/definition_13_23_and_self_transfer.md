**2. If the ticket is over HK$8,000, will they pay HK$8,000?**

**Not unless they write it.** Three live readings:

- **A (ceiling):** they pay HK$8,000; you pay the rest. This is what you want. They have not said it.
- **B:** they raise the cap to your fare. Unlikely without asking.
- **C:** a ticket over HK$8,000 is refused **in full**. This is why you do not buy first.

A fourth cut sits inside “lowest economy.” Even under A, they can pay `min(your ticket, cheapest 15/18 economy including 1-stops, HK$8,000)`. Google Flights on 22 Aug 2026 (USD, bags often extra):

| Search | Cheapest shown | CX/BA **nonstop** |
|---|---|---|
| **15–18 Oct** (circular pair) | **US$750** (~HK$5,900) Qatar 1-stop | US$1,111–1,174 (~HK$8,700–9,200) |
| **13–23 Oct** | **US$715–799** 1-stop | **US$1,071–1,079** (~HK$8,350–8,400) |
| **14–23 Oct** | US$715–799 1-stop | US$1,071–1,079 |
| **15–23 Oct** (July pattern) | US$735–857 1-stop | Daytime CX **US$1,079**; overnight CX **US$1,248** |

So: a **CX/BA nonstop 13/14/15–23** is a bit over HK$8,000. A **1-stop on 15–18** is **under** HK$8,000. If they use the circular pair as the comparator, you might get ~HK$5,900, not HK$8,000. If they accept your actual 13–23 CX ticket under Option A, you get HK$8,000 and pay ~HK$400. **Get that in writing.** July’s “quotation is fine” does not cover a new fare above the cap.
