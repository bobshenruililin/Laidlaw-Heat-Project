# Lowest economy airfare — the definition to follow

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Use:** Search and, if they confirm, buy against this definition only. Do not invent a looser one.

Horizons wrote “only the lowest economy airfare will be approved” and named one itinerary. They did not define “lowest” as “cheapest ticket that happens to overlap the conference.” Follow the circular’s date pair unless they write a different pair.

---

## The definition

**Lowest economy airfare** for this claim is the cheapest **bookable** ticket that meets all of the following on the **day you purchase** (or, for the email, the day of the screenshot):

| Axis | Include | Exclude |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin | Economy / main cabin / coach, lowest remaining economy bucket | Premium economy, business, first |
| Airports | Hong Kong International (**HKG**) both ends, London **Heathrow (LHR)** both ends | Gatwick, City, Stansted, Luton, Manchester, open-jaw |
| Outbound date | **15 October 2026** | “Any day that gets me there for Friday” as the search itself |
| Return date | **18 October 2026**, flight departing LHR **after 14:00** local (the conference’s last session) | 23 October as the search that defines “lowest” |
| Routing | HKG–LHR–HKG, fewest stops (direct if it exists and is cheaper) | Multi-city, long European stopovers that raise the price |
| Price | Total **including tax**, converted to HKD if the site is not in HKD | A “from” banner, a July screenshot, a fare you cannot check out |
| Seats | A fare you can actually pay for today | Sold-out example; waitlist; a quote that fails at payment |

Attendance still has to be possible on that ticket: land in London **before 17:00 on Friday 16 October**, and do not leave LHR **before 14:00 on Sunday 18 October**. The circular’s 15 / 18 October pair is how they budgeted that constraint. It is not “lowest fare among all October dates that cover 16–18 October.”

---

## What “lowest” is not

| Wrong search | Why it fails this circular |
|---|---|
| Cheapest ticket in October that covers the weekend | That is a leisure search. Finance compares against **named travel dates**. |
| Cheapest of 15/18 vs 15/19 vs 14/19 vs 15/23, then pick the min | Mixing date pairs without their written comparator. 15/23 was allowed in July only because that quote was **under** the 15/18 example. |
| Cheapest you saw on 24 July (HK$6,900) | That fare is gone. “Lowest” is at **purchase time**, not at circular time. |
| Cheapest to “London” | Circular named **Heathrow**. |
| Cheapest including premium economy because economy is sold out | Then economy on those dates is sold out. That is an over-cap / no-go question, not a cabin upgrade. |

---

## If 15 / 18 October has no workable return after 14:00

Then the circular’s date pair is broken, and you do **not** get to pick a new pair yourself.

1. Screenshot the 15 Oct / 18 Oct search (sold out, no evening return, or price).
2. Screenshot the next calendar-feasible pair: **15 October out, 19 October back**.
3. In the email, ask them to **name** which pair is the comparator. Until they name it, the comparator remains 15 / 18.

Do not treat 15 / 23 October as the definition. If you still want those extra days after they confirm, reimbursement is the **lesser of** (your 15/23 ticket) and (the named 15/18 or 15/19 lowest economy) and (HK$8,000), unless they write something else.

---

## How to search (one pass)

1. One website (Google Flights, airline, or a Hong Kong OTA). Economy. HKG–LHR–HKG.
2. Dates **15 Oct → 18 Oct**. Filter returns after 14:00 if the tool allows; otherwise discard earlier-Sunday options by eye.
3. Sort by price. Record the cheapest **bookable** total including tax.
4. Repeat **15 Oct → 19 Oct** as a fallback screenshot only.
5. Repeat **15 Oct → 23 Oct** only to show it is no longer the cheap option.
6. Do not buy. Attach the screenshots to [`to_horizons.md`](to_horizons.md).

That 15 / 18 number **is** “the lowest economy fare” under the circular. If it is above HK$8,000, ask Horizons whether reimbursement is still **up to HK$8,000** (you pay the rest) or whether they will not reimburse an over-cap ticket.
