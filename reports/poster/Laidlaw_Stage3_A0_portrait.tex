\sechead{INTRODUCTION}
\body
Hong Kong is warming fastest at night: hot nights at the Hong Kong Observatory
rose from 10 days in 2013 to a peak of 61 in 2021, while winter cold days have
not disappeared. For the growing number of people living with diabetes or
hypertension --- a group at high cardiovascular risk --- adaptation planning
must budget for the hospital burden, not only mortality. Whether monthly heat
and cold burdens track first cardiovascular hospitalisations in this group has
not been quantified.

\vspace{6mm}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_poster_weather.pdf}\par
\vspace{2.5mm}
\begin{flushleft}
\captext{Official Hong Kong Observatory extremes at Headquarters. Hot nights
rose about fivefold over the decade; cold days recurred every winter --- the
exposure setting for this study.}
\end{flushleft}
\end{minipage}\hfill\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\textwidth}
\sechead{OBJECTIVE}
\begin{questionbox}
\fontsize{28}{35.5}\selectfont\color{Navy}
Are monthly temperature and extreme-day burdens associated with first
\textbf{coronary heart disease (CHD)} or \textbf{heart failure (HF)}
hospitalisations in a high-risk Hong Kong cohort?\par
\end{questionbox}

\sechead{METHODS}
\centering

  {12 negative-binomial\\models:\\2 outcomes \texttimes{} 6 exposures};
\node[flownode,text width=0.215\linewidth] (checks) at (0.765\linewidth,-18mm)
  {Season + trend control;\\4 standard-error methods;\\Benjamini--Hochberg \textit{q}};
\draw[flowarrow] (wx.east) -- (panel.west |- wx.east);
\draw[flowarrow] (ha.east) -- (panel.west |- ha.east);
\draw[flowarrow] (panel.east) -- (models.west);
\draw[flowarrow] (models.east) -- (checks.west);
\end{tikzpicture}\par
\vspace{6mm}
\begin{flushleft}
\body
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Window and weather:} 132 territory-months, Jan 2013 -- Dec 2023;
        daily records and official extreme-day definitions from the Hong Kong
        Observatory.
  \item \textbf{Outcomes:} governed Hospital Authority aggregate counts of
        \emph{first hospitalisation after a first recorded CHD or HF diagnosis}
        in people with type 2 diabetes and/or hypertension --- 156{,}156 CHD and
        29{,}681 HF events. Admission cause is not recorded.
  \item \textbf{Models:} 2 outcomes \texttimes{} 6 exposures = 12 pre-specified
        negative-binomial regressions with calendar-month indicators, a trend
        spline, and a days-in-month offset.
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty:} four standard-error methods per contrast;
        Benjamini--Hochberg \textit{q} across all 12 comparisons.
\end{itemize}
\end{flushleft}
\vspace{2mm}
\notetext{\textbf{Definitions.} Hot night: minimum 28\,\textdegree C or higher;
very hot day: maximum 33\,\textdegree C or higher; cold day: minimum
12\,\textdegree C or lower (HKO definitions). Estimates are count ratios per 5
extreme days or per 1\,\textdegree C.}
\end{minipage}

\vspace{4mm}
\body
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CHD \texttimes{} hot nights:} about \textbf{2.2\% higher} monthly
        count per five additional hot nights --- but the interval
        \emph{includes 1} under two of four standard-error methods, so the
        signal is uncertainty-sensitive.
  \item \textbf{HF \texttimes{} cold days:} about \textbf{7.3\% higher} monthly
        count per five additional cold days --- the interval excludes 1 under
        all four methods.
  \item The other ten contrasts sit near 1. \textbf{All 12 Benjamini--Hochberg
        \textit{q}-values exceed 0.19}: after correction for 12 comparisons,
        both signals remain exploratory.
\end{itemize}

\sechead{INTERPRETATION / LIMITATIONS}
\body
\begin{itemize}
  \item All 12 \textit{q}-values exceed 0.19 --- these are \textbf{exploratory
        signals}, not confirmed effects.
  \item The CHD hot-night estimate is \textbf{sensitive to how uncertainty is
        computed}; the HF cold-day estimate is stable across methods.
  \item Monthly, territory-level data with no recorded admission cause support
        \textbf{no causal or principal-diagnosis claim}; a stroke extract was
        not delivered, so stroke is not reported.
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}\hfill\begin{minipage}[t]{0.485\linewidth}
\sechead{CONCLUSION}
\begin{takeawaybox}
\fontsize{27}{34.5}\selectfont
Monthly data suggest \textbf{different thermal patterns} for CHD and HF first
hospitalisations --- hot nights for CHD, cold days for HF --- but the evidence
is \textbf{exploratory, not confirmatory}: no contrast survived correction for
twelve comparisons. The project leaves a \textbf{reproducible monthly
framework} and a clear next requirement: governed \textbf{daily or cohort data
with recorded admission cause}.\par
\end{takeawaybox}

\vspace{5mm}
\notetext{\textbf{What would strengthen this:} linked daily weather and
admission records with cause of admission, so the monthly burden patterns seen
here can be tested at the resolution at which heat and cold act.}
\end{minipage}
